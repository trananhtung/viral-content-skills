**Why this works:**

- **Hook = curiosity gap (Unexpected).** "Don't survive the honeymoon" breaks the schema that all personalization is equal and opens a gap the next two lines close. The surprise is post-dictable — everyone has owned a shirt whose print flaked — and it *is* the core message, not a gimmick.
- **One core idea, stated as a proverb (Simple).** Stitched, not printed. Everything else is evidence for it, and the brand tagline "your names, made to stay" is literally the same sentence, so the ad and the brand reinforce each other instead of competing.
- **Testable credential (Credible).** "Run your thumb across your new last name and you'll feel the ridges" is a Wendy's-style *try-it-yourself* proof the reader can verify the second the package arrives — far stronger than asserting "premium quality."
- **Concrete over abstract (Concrete).** "'Mrs.' has lost its M," "the airport," "every Sunday" replace claim-words like durable and high-quality. The failure is something you can picture; so is the fix.
- **Identity + high-arousal emotion (Emotional).** The felt stake isn't a sweatshirt, it's *your new last name* being erased — mild anxiety flipping to relief, aimed at the newlywed identity rather than at a discount-hunter.
- **Conversion framing (Practical Value).** Under $100, so the percentage is louder than the dollar amount (Rule of 100): "30% off" leads, "$69.95 (was $99.99)" supplies the reference point. Free personalization, free shipping, and 5 options clear the last hesitations, then a single CTA — one ask, not three.
- **A trigger that fires later (Triggers).** "Wash it every Sunday" welds the product to laundry day, the exact recurring moment when a peeling print would otherwise be remembered — so the ad keeps re-advertising itself after the scroll.
