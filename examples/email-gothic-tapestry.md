**Why this works:**

- **Hook opens a curiosity gap (Unexpected):** the subject names something the reader has genuinely never audited — the wall in the background of every photo — and the body closes the gap in the second line. It's post-dictable: "of course."
- **The core is one sentence, and it leads (Simple):** rentals leave, your names stay. Everything else in the email serves that spine; no second or third message competing with it.
- **Concrete throughout (Concrete):** a van on Sunday morning, folded linen, a wall in your first apartment, five years. Nothing to picture is left abstract — no "unique," no "keepsake quality," no "unforgettable."
- **Emotion is high-arousal and identity-shaped (Emotional):** anticipation of the photo plus real deadline anxiety ("stacking up in the production queue now"), aimed at a couple who already decided their wedding wouldn't look like everyone else's.
- **Credibility is testable and honest (Credible):** the wedding-date guarantee is a claim the reader can hold you to, and "5.0 from 4 couples" states the small sample instead of hiding it — specificity reads as truth where "loved by brides everywhere" reads as filler.
- **Value framed for a sub-$100 item (Practical Value / Rule of 100):** percentage discount leads because 17% looks larger than $5.04 below the $100 threshold; the free-shipping floor is stated as a fact, not a nag.
- **One CTA, phrased as the action itself:** "Put your names on it" is the product's whole promise and the brand tagline in verb form — not "Shop now."
